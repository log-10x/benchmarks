Drain's clustering is stable under reordering. The published shuffle result
reproduces here and stands. The identifier Drain returns alongside it is an
arrival counter, so it tracks discovery order instead of line content.

Cap pressure leaves the split intact. At max_clusters=500, with eviction live,
the template holds at 0.9848 and the integer falls further, to 0.0000.

So hashing the template is the correct repair, and it is the one log10x
recommends to anyone staying on Drain. What the hash cannot repair is the
template itself moving, which bench/replay.py shows in four lines, and the
frequency-dependent disagreement between two instances of Drain fed different
halves of one stream, which the split-instance benchmark in this repo measures.
